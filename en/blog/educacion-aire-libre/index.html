Outdoor education: why the mountains are a classroom too

Autonomy, teamwork and a bond with the land: what children and young people learn on rivers, lakes and snow.

Outdoor education uses nature as a place to learn. It's not only about learning to paddle, ride or ski: it's about raising people who can know, enjoy and care for their surroundings.

What children and young people learn

  • Autonomy and confidence: solving real challenges with an instructor's support.
  • Risk management: reading the weather, water and terrain, and making good decisions.
  • Teamwork: on a raft, a rope team or a kayak group, everyone depends on everyone.
  • A bond with nature: what we know and enjoy, we protect.

Why it matters locally

Many children grow up next to the volcano, the lake and the river without ever exploring them. Giving them access to their own land strengthens local identity, opens paths to careers such as guide or instructor, and trains those who will care for the destination in the future.

How we do it

  • Technical progression by age and level.
  • Instructors with teaching and technical training, and safety protocols on every outing.
  • Kayak touring, mountaineering, mountain biking, sport climbing and skiing.
  • Leave No Trace as part of every day.

This approach comes from the Water and Mountain School in Choshuenco, Punahue and Paillahuinte, where we also created its ski school. If you are a school, municipality or organisation, message us and we'll design a program for your group.
